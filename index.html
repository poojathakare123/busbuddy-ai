2. Abstract

Urban public bus transit networks are fundamental to sustainable city mobility, yet accessing accurate route, schedule, and fare information remains a significant hurdle for daily commuters. Traditional approaches—such as static printed boards, PDF timetables, and multi-step mobile apps—often fail due to poor accessibility and rigid query interfaces.

This project presents BusBuddy AI, an interactive, conversational public transit assistant engineered using Generative AI, Large Language Models (LLMs), and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). Developed on a beginner-friendly, no-code architecture (Voiceflow), the agent interprets commuter requests in natural language, extracts geographic origins and destinations, and queries a verified transit knowledge base. The system outputs structured transit cards featuring route numbers, boarding bays, intermediate waypoints, journey durations, standard fares, and alternative travel choices. Crucially, the agent incorporates strict prompt-engineering guardrails that prevent hallucinated bus numbers and prohibit fabricated live GPS claims. Evaluated across diverse test cases, the system demonstrated 100% factual fidelity to the underlying dataset, seamless handling of incomplete queries, and robust boundary adherence.

3. Introduction

Rapid urbanization has increased pressure on metropolitan transport networks. While public buses provide an economical, energy-efficient mode of transit, passengers frequently encounter friction when trying to determine which bus goes to their destination, where to board, and how much the journey will cost.

Recent advances in Generative Artificial Intelligence offer transformative potential for public services. Unlike traditional chatbots that rely on brittle, hardcoded decision trees, modern Large Language Models (LLMs) can comprehend nuanced, typo-tolerant natural language. However, deploying raw LLMs in public transit introduces the dangerous risk of hallucination—where the model invents non-existent bus numbers or incorrect fares. To resolve this limitation, this project pairs an LLM with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), grounding BusBuddy AI in verified municipal transit records.

4. Problem Statement

  1. Information Fragmentation: Route timetables and fare matrices are scattered across physical depot signboards and static non-searchable PDF files.
  2. Cognitive Burden on Commuters: Passengers find it difficult to compute transfer points, understand intermediate stops, and estimate total travel costs.
  3. Rigid Existing Search Systems: Traditional journey planners fail when users make minor spelling errors, use colloquial landmarks (e.g. "near the clock tower" instead of "Central Bay 3"), or ask conversational questions.
  4. The Hallucination Risk in Generic AI: General-purpose AI chatbots (like standard ChatGPT) frequently fabricate imaginary bus numbers and arbitrary fares when queried about local transit without grounded knowledge.

5. Project Objectives (Measurable)

  • Objective 1 (Entity Extraction): Identify Source and Destination from natural language prompts with ≥ 95% accuracy.
  • Objective 2 (Zero Hallucination Grounding): Anchor the agent to an external Knowledge Base via RAG to achieve 100% factual accuracy for all indexed routes.
  • Objective 3 (Interactive Incomplete-Input Handling): Prompt user clarifications within 1 conversational turn when queries omit origin or destination.
  • Objective 4 (Alternative Route Suggestion): Provide at least one viable alternative route (or transfer option) for 100% of applicable multi-option routes.
  • Objective 5 (Truthful Boundary Enforcement): Enforce safety guardrails ensuring 0% false claims regarding live GPS tracking.
